Part 2 · Ad audit

Five Nanobag ads, picked apart

Where the five ads lose the buyer

Too salesy

Three of the five lean on mission statements or spec-reading. People know ads are promotional, but these sound like a pitch from the first line.

Instead: sound like a friend who found something. Show it, say less.

No wow, no impulse buy

The moment that makes someone want one, a bag vanishing into a pocket or a pouch turning into a backpack, comes late in Ads 1 and 4, is only a caption in Ad 2 and is missing in Ad 3. Only Ad 5 shows it in one unbroken shot.

Instead: make the first seconds the wow, in one shot.

Too long, sometimes

Ads 1 and 2 run 45s and 40s. Ads 3, 4 and 5 are within 30s (23, 29 and 14), so length is a problem in only some of them. The shortest, Ad 5, is also the one with the clearest hook.

Instead: trim Ads 1 and 2 to 15 to 20 seconds and keep the rest tight, with the hook up front.

Hooks that don't work

Three of five open on brand talk, a version number or a collage. Ad 2 has a fair hook but states it instead of asking, and Ad 5 is the exception.

Instead: open on something that makes the viewer ask a question: a number that looks wrong, a pouch that becomes a bag, a crowd doing one thing.

Too many steps to buy

Not every ad has a button, and the ones that do send people to the homepage instead of the purchase page. Every extra click is a chance to lose the buyer, so fewer steps is better.

Instead: send every button straight to the purchase page.

The final seconds are wasted

Ads end on a logo, a web address, a spoken line or a smile. The last seconds are the final persuading message, and none of them is spent on a reason to buy.

Instead: end on one reason to buy: the number, the scarcity or a closing line.

Proof shows up, but quietly

The best proof, the 0.05 mm gauge, the 24 g scale and the self-fold, gets a plain caption and sits in the middle or back of the ad.

Instead: one proof per ad, early, with the number big on screen.

Not enough real people using it

Ads 4 and 5 have a creator feel, but the rest lean on studio shots, stock footage or captions, and Ad 2 claims a sell-out without showing anyone buying. People buy when they see others using it or buying it.

Instead: lean on natural UGC: a real person showing how they use it and saying why in their own words, not reading features.

Same reels, same captions

Several of the ads reuse the same core clips, the pouch pull-out, the unfurl and the pocket tuck, under near-identical captions. The footage is fine; the lack of variation is the gap, because nothing shows which caption is doing the work.

Instead: keep the reel, change one caption at a time and run the versions side by side.
